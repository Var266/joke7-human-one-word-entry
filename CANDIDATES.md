# Candidates — Your Human in One Word

Gate: exactly one token, 1–30 UTF-8 chars, no spaces, no explanation in the entry itself.
Brief priority: (1) one word describing the human the entrant works for (boss/client/manager);
(2) funny/wry, not a flat label; (3) fits that human — reads true for a boss/client/manager,
not a random insult or stock praise; (4) vivid & specific, memorable — not flat like
fine/busy/okay; (5) lands on its own without explanation.

## Candidate pool (12)

1. `micromanager` (12) — the classic hovering boss.
2. `bottleneck` (10) — every approval, decision, and reply must squeeze through one person.
3. `gaslighter` (10) — "I never said that" as a management style.
4. `mansplainer` (11) — explains your own job back to you, slowly.
5. `delegator` (9) — hands everything down, keeps the credit up.
6. `overpromiser` (12) — promises the client the moon, then hands you the rocket bill.
7. `perfectionist` (13) — nothing is ever quite done.
8. `procrastinator` (14) — feedback arrives at the deadline, or after it.
9. `workaholic` (10) — lives at work, expects the same rent from you.
10. `scopecreeper` (12) — the brief grows a new limb every week.
11. `firefighter` (11) — only visible when something is already burning.
12. `multitasker` (11) — twelve tabs, zero finished threads.

All 12 are single tokens, ≤30 chars, no spaces — all pass the gate on form.

## Pairwise evaluation

**Round 1 — flat/stock elimination (criteria 2 & 4):**
- `delegator` vs `workaholic`: both read as stock labels / mild praise. `delegator` is
  almost a compliment; `workaholic` is a résumé cliché. Both lose on wryness. Eliminated.
- `perfectionist` vs `multitasker`: both are flat personality labels that could describe
  anyone, not specifically the human you work for. Lose on fit (3) and vividness (4). Eliminated.
- `procrastinator` vs `firefighter`: `procrastinator` is generic; `firefighter` at least
  paints a scene (appears only in a crisis). `firefighter` advances, `procrastinator` out.

**Round 2 — fit for boss/client/manager (criterion 3):**
- `mansplainer` vs `gaslighter`: both vivid, but both read as random insults that could
  target anyone anywhere; neither is specifically true of the approval-chain human.
  `gaslighter` is the sharper of the two. `gaslighter` advances narrowly.
- `micromanager` vs `scopecreeper`: `micromanager` fits a manager perfectly but is the
  dictionary definition of a bad boss — a flat label (2). `scopecreeper` is more specific
  to client work (the ever-growing brief) and wittier. `scopecreeper` advances.
- `overpromiser` vs `firefighter`: `overpromiser` describes a very specific client/boss
  failure mode (sells the moon, delivers you the bill). `firefighter` is vivid but can
  read as praise (the hero who saves the day). `overpromiser` advances on wryness.

**Semifinal — vivid & memorable, lands alone (criteria 4 & 5):**
- `gaslighter` vs `scopecreeper`: `gaslighter` lands instantly but is a generic insult
  (3). `scopecreeper` needs a beat to parse as a coined compound and slightly blurs
  "one word" into two glued words. Neither is clean. Both are beaten below.
- `overpromiser` vs `bottleneck`: `overpromiser` is specific and wry, but it describes
  a sales habit — truest for a client-facing boss. `bottleneck` describes the universal
  experience of working for a human: work, approvals, answers, and deadlines all queue
  behind one person who is simultaneously the only route through and the reason nothing
  moves. It is a metaphor, so it is vivid (4); it is wry rather than a flat label (2);
  it is true of bosses, clients, and managers alike (3); and it needs zero explanation (5).

**Final — `bottleneck` vs `micromanager` (the stock champion):**
`micromanager` wins on instant recognition but loses on the brief's explicit demand:
"luc­u/wry, bukan label datar" and "vivid & spesifik, memorable — bukan kata flat".
It is the first word anyone would submit. `bottleneck` says the same workplace truth
with an image — one narrow neck, everything stuck behind it — and that image is what
makes it memorable in a pairwise comparison.

## Selected

**`bottleneck`** — 10 characters, one token, no spaces.

Why it wins: it describes the human the entrant works for (the single approval point
every task must pass), it is wry rather than a flat label, it reads true for a boss,
client, or manager without being a random insult or stock praise, the metaphor makes it
vivid and memorable, and it lands completely on its own with no explanation needed.
